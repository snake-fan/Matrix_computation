\textbf{Solution.}

First, we find the eigenvalues by solving the characteristic equation $|\mathbf{A} - \lambda \mathbf{I}| = 0$:

$$\det
\begin{bmatrix} 2-\lambda & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2-\lambda & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & a-\lambda

$$
(1-\lambda)(\lambda^2 - (3+a)\lambda + 3a - 2) = 0
$$

and $\lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 3a-2$, $\lambda_2 \cdot \lambda_3 = (3+a) / 2$

Since $\A$ is PSD, we need to ensure:
$$
\lambda_2 + \lambda_3 \ge 0 \quad and \quad \lambda_2 \cdot \lambda_3 \ge 0
$$

Thus we can get:
$$
a \ge \frac{2}{3}
$$

\section*{Problem 2. (Positive Semideﬁnite Matrices)}

\subsection*{1)}

\textbf{Solution.}

\textbf{Proof symmetric:}

$$
A^T = (I + \alpha \alpha^T)^T = I^T + (\alpha \alpha^T)^T = I + (\alpha^T)^T \alpha^T = I + \alpha \alpha^T = A
$$

\textbf{Proof PD:}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\x^T \A \x &= \x^T (I + \alpha \alpha^T) \x \\
&= \x^T \x + (\x^T \alpha) (\alpha^T \x)    \\
&= \|\x\|_2 + \|\alpha^T \x\|_2
\end{aligned}$$

Thus, when $\x \ne 0$, $\x^T \A \x > 0$, i.e. $\A$ is PD matrix.

\subsection*{2)}

\textbf{Solution.}

\textbf{Case 1: The eigenvector is parallel to $\alpha$}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A \alpha &= (I + \alpha \alpha^T) \alpha \\
&= \alpha + \alpha (\alpha^T \alpha)    \\
&= (1 + \beta)\alpha
\end{aligned}$$

Thus, $\lambda_1 = 1 + \beta$ is an eigenvalue with eigenvector $\alpha$.
Since $\alpha$ is a single vector, the algebraic multiplicity is 1.

\textbf{Case 2: The eigenvectors are orthogonal to $\alpha$}

Let $v$ be any non-zero vector satisfy $\alpha^T v = 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A v &= (I + \alpha \alpha^T) v \\
&= v + \alpha (\alpha^T v) \\
&= v
\end{aligned}$$
Thus, $\lambda_2 = 1$ is an eigenvalue.

Since the subspace orthogonal to $\alpha$ has dimension $n-1$,
there are $n-1$ linearly independent eigenvectors associated with this eigenvalue.
The algebraic multiplicity is $n-1$.

\section*{Problem 3. (Application of Schur Complement)}

\textbf{Solution.}

We will use the Schur Complement to solve this problem.

$$
M =
\begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ B^T & C
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then if $ A \succ 0 $, we just need
$$S = C - B^T A^{-1} B \succeq 0$$
and we will get $M \succeq 0$.

To reduce the computation, we move rows and columns containing a to the end.

Then we can get

$$X =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & \mathbf{2} & 1 & 0 & 0 & -1 \\
\mathbf{2} & \mathbf{a} & \mathbf{2} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{2} \\
1 & \mathbf{2} & 2 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & \mathbf{0} & 0 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & \mathbf{0} & 0 & 0 & 2 & 0 \\
-1 & \mathbf{2} & 0 & 0 & 0 & 2
\end{bmatrix}$$

$$A =
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1 & 0 & 0 & -1 \\
1 & 2 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 2 & 0 \\
-1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 2
\end{bmatrix}$$

$$B =
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 2 & 0 & 0 & 2
\end{bmatrix}^T$$

$$C = a$$

To make $X$ be PSD matrix, we just need to make sure $ A \succ 0 $ and $S = C - B^T A^{-1} B \succeq 0$.

\textbf{Step1. Prove} $ A \succ 0 $

Since the 3rd, 4th row of $A$ only have diagonal entry 2, we ignore them to simplify the problem.

Then we focus on
$$A_{core} =
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 2
\end{bmatrix}$$

Since:
\begin{itemize}
\item $2>0$
\item $\det{\left(
\begin{matrix}
2 & 1   \\
1 & 2   \\
\end{matrix}\right)} = 3 > 0
$
\item $\det{\left(
\begin{matrix}
2 & 1 & -1   \\
1 & 2 & 0  \\
-1 & 0 & 2  \\
\end{matrix}\right)} = 4 > 0
$
\end{itemize}
we can get that $ A \succ 0 $.

\textbf{Step2. Prove} $S = C - B^T A^{-1} B \succeq 0$

Let$y = A^{-1} B$, then $Ay = B$.

We can solve the line system an get that:
$$
y = [2, 0, 0, 0, 2]^T
$$

Thus,
\begin{align*}
S &= C - B^T A^{-1} B  \\
S &= a - B^T y  \\
&= a -
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 2 & 0 & 0 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 2
\end{bmatrix}  \\
&= a - 8
\end{align*}

If we want $S \ge 0$, we just need $a \ge 8$

\section*{Problem 4. (Matrix Inequality, Matrix Norm)}

\subsection*{1)}

\textbf{Solution.}

First, we can easily get
$$\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X}) = \min_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2=1} \|\mathbf{Xv}\|_2$$

Since $\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{I}_r$ is a real symmetric matrix,
we can get:
$$\|\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{I}_r\|_2 = \max_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2=1} |\mathbf{v}^\top (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{I}_r) \mathbf{v}| = \tau$$

Then,
\begin{align*}
\max_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2=1} |\mathbf{v}^\top (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{I}_r) \mathbf{v}| &= \tau  \\
|\mathbf{v}^\top (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{I}_r) \mathbf{v}| &\le \tau  \\
|\mathbf{v}^\top \mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X} \mathbf{v}- 1| &\le \tau    \\
|\|\mathbf{Xv}\|_2^2 - 1| &\leq \tau 
\end{align*}
Thus,
$$1 - \tau \leq \|\mathbf{Xv}\|_2^2 \leq 1 + \tau$$

Since $1 - \tau \leq \|\mathbf{Xv}\|_2^2$, we can get:
$$\min_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2=1} \|\mathbf{Xv}\|_2^2 \geq 1 - \tau$$
i.e.,
$$\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X})^2 \geq 1 - \tau$$

$$\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X}) \geq \sqrt{1 - \tau}$$

Since $0 \leq \tau < 1$, we know that $\sqrt{1-\tau} \geq 1-\tau$.

Therefore:$$\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X}) \geq 1 - \tau$$

\subsection*{2)}

\textbf{Solution.}

$$\sigma_1(\mathbf{E}) = \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{E}^\top \mathbf{E})}$$

First, we get $\mathbf{E}^\top \mathbf{E}$
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{E}^\top \mathbf{E} &= (\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}^\top)^\top (\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}^\top)    \\
&= \|a\|_2 \mathbf{b} \mathbf{b}^\top
\end{align*}

Since $\mathbf{E}^\top \mathbf{E} = \|a\|_2 \mathbf{b} \mathbf{b}^\top$ is a rank-1 matrix,
we can get that $\mathbf{E}^\top \mathbf{E}$ only have one none zero eigenvalue.

Consider the vector $\mathbf{b}$ as a candidate eigenvector for the matrix 
$\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b}^\top$:
$$(\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b}^\top) \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} (\mathbf{b}^\top \mathbf{b}) = \mathbf{b} (\|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2) = \|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2 \mathbf{b}$$

This shows that $\mathbf{b}$ is an eigenvector of $\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b}^\top$ with respect to the eigenvalue $\|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2$.

Thus, the non-zero eigenvalue of the full matrix $\mathbf{E}^\top \mathbf{E} = \|\mathbf{a}\|_2^2 (\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b}^\top)$ is:
$$\lambda_{\max} = \|\mathbf{a}\|_2^2 \cdot \|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2$$

$$\sigma_1(\mathbf{E}) = \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{E}^\top \mathbf{E})} = \|\mathbf{a}\|_2 \cdot \|\mathbf{b}\|_2$$
